**MEMO**

**To:** Executive Committee
**From:** People Analytics
**Date:** 22 April 2025
**Subject:** Four-day week pilot: results and recommendation

**Summary.** Our 13-week pilot of a four-day week in Customer Engineering raised productivity by 18% and cut attrition by almost two thirds, with no loss of customer satisfaction. We recommend rolling the four-day week out to all 1,240 engineers from the start of Q3 2025.

**1. Pilot design**

The pilot ran for the 13 weeks from 6 January to 4 April 2025. Pilot engineers worked 32 hours over four days at unchanged pay. In November 2024 every team in Customer Engineering was invited to apply, and all seven teams that applied were accepted: 61 engineers in total. The applicants were an enthusiastic group and included all three teams that won last year's Delivery Excellence award. The remaining 12 teams (174 engineers) kept the usual five-day week and serve as the comparison group.

Both groups were measured over exactly the same 13 calendar weeks, so seasonality, holidays and release cycles affected them equally.

Customer Engineering is a fair proxy for engineering as a whole: the 2024 work-mix study found that every engineering organisation uses the same ticketing process and has a similar mix of feature, maintenance and support work, and similar on-call duties.

**2. How we measured productivity**

Our productivity measure is tickets closed per engineer per week, taken directly from the ticketing system. The definition of "closed" and the script that counts closed tickets were identical for both groups, and Internal Audit reviewed both before the pilot began. Tickets reopened within 14 days were not counted as closed for either group.

To help pilot teams adapt to the shorter week, their agile coaches encouraged them to split work into smaller tickets that fit within four days. Over the pilot, the average size of a pilot team's ticket fell from 5.0 story points to 3.4 story points. The comparison group's average ticket size stayed at 5.0 story points throughout.

**3. Results**

*Productivity.* Pilot engineers closed an average of 9.2 tickets per week, against 7.8 for the comparison group: 18% more. The effect was stable across the pilot rather than a short-lived burst of enthusiasm: the pilot's weekly average was 9.1 in weeks 1–4, 9.3 in weeks 5–9 and 9.2 in weeks 10–13. A test on weekly team-level averages puts the difference well outside what chance would produce (p = 0.01).

*Customer satisfaction.* The average customer satisfaction (CSAT) score on tickets handled by pilot teams was 4.5 out of 5 (from 1,900 survey responses), against 4.4 for the comparison group (5,200 responses). The four-day week did not hurt customers.

*Attrition.* Only 2 of the 61 pilot engineers resigned during the pilot, an attrition rate of 3.3%. The company's attrition rate for 2024 was 9% per year. The four-day week therefore cut attrition by almost two thirds, which matters because each departure costs us roughly $90,000 in recruiting and ramp-up time.

*Engagement.* In the end-of-pilot survey, 57 of the 61 pilot engineers (93%) said they would like to keep the four-day week.

**4. Recommendation**

Because the pilot raised productivity by 18% and cut attrition by almost two thirds, we recommend extending the four-day week to all 1,240 engineers from 1 July 2025. An 18% productivity gain across 1,240 engineers is equivalent to adding about 223 engineers of capacity at no extra salary cost. We propose a review after two quarters, with the option to return any team to a five-day week if its results fall short.
